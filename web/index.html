What Buy and Strong Buy mean

Stocks: Buy = 1 of 3 dip signals, Strong Buy = 2 or more, Panic Buy = a deep dip (RSI(2) under 5) in an uptrend while the VIX is spiking: rare, and the best-tested setup (+2% over 5 days, 70% winners since 2021). Crypto: Buy = 3 of 4 trend signals with none against, Strong Buy = all 4 plus Bitcoin's weekly trend up. Alerts go out for Strong Buys and Panic Buys.

          How it works

          Signal Desk checks your watchlist on daily price bars: stocks every 30 minutes while the market is open, crypto every hour. A few simple, tested models each vote on whether to buy. When enough agree you see a Buy; when they all line up you see a Strong Buy and get a Telegram alert. Stocks and crypto use different models because they behave differently.

          Stocks: buy the dip in an uptrend

          Three models look for a short, sharp drop in a stock that is still in a long-term uptrend (above its 200-day average). Those dips have tended to bounce within days.

          RSI dip
          The 2-day RSI is below 10: two very oversold days. It stays on until the price closes back above its 5-day average.
          Low close
          The day closed in the bottom 20% of its high-to-low range.
          VIX panic
          The market's 1-month fear gauge (VIX) is above its 3-month version, which happens in sell-offs. It applies to every stock at once.

          Buy = 1 of 3 · Strong Buy = 2 or 3 · Panic Buy = the 2-day RSI is under 5, the stock is above its 200-day average, and the VIX is above its 3-month version (a market-wide scare). These models only say when to buy, so stocks never show Sell.

          Crypto: ride the trend

          Coins have tended to keep moving in the direction they're already going. Four trend models check that from different angles.

          Momentum
          The price is higher than 28 days ago.
          MAs
          The price is above at least 2 of its 20-, 50- and 100-day averages.
          Breakout
          At least half of nine breakout systems (looking back 5 to 360 days) are in an uptrend.
          BTC
          Bitcoin is above its 50-day average. When Bitcoin is weak, most coins are.
          BTC weekly
          Bitcoin's weekly trend (ribbon blue and above its 40-week average). Not a vote: it decides whether an all-4 reading counts as Strong Buy or just Buy.

          Buy = 3 of 4 and none pointing down · Strong Buy = all 4 with Bitcoin's weekly trend up · Sell = most point down (can't happen while Bitcoin is above its 50-day average).

          Also on each ticker page

          For context only. None of these change the signal.

          • Layer plan: prices 1, 2, 3… average daily moves (ATR) below and above the 50-day average, for adding or trimming in steps.
          • Position size: suggests a smaller position when something swings more than usual (guide: 30% a year for stocks, 60% for crypto).
          • InvestAnswers-style view: the ribbon, ATR buy/sell line and all-time-high levels, rebuilt from your TradingView screenshots.
          • Congress trades: stock trades members of Congress reported in official House and Senate filings.
          • What's driving it: once a day, the latest quarter's sales and earnings growth, margins, analyst targets and next earnings date are set against the price move, with the latest headlines. Built from numbers only, so it shows what changed but can't know every reason.
          • Long-term (weekly) trend: Uptrend when the weekly ribbon is blue and the price is above its 40-week average, Downtrend when both point down. On the S&P 500 since 2005, staying out during weekly downtrends cut the worst drop from −55% to about −21%, for roughly 3% a year less return. On single stocks it mostly cost return, so it's shown as information (the "Weekly ↑/↓" tags, the market bar and the Weekly chart), not as a buy/sell signal.

          How it was tested

          • Up to 10 years of daily prices (2017 to now) for your stocks and the largest coins. 18 models were tried.
          • Chosen on the past, judged on the future: the rules were picked using 2017–2020 only, then checked on 2021–2026, which they never saw. The numbers below are from that unseen period.
          • Trading costs included: 0.05% per trade for stocks, 0.25% for crypto.
          • No peeking: each day's signal uses only prices up to that day's close.
          • Luck check (t): how unlikely a result is to be chance. About 2 or more is reasonably convincing; under 1 is noise.

          Tested and left out

          • 4-hour and 1-hour signals: no model held up on intraday bars, so those charts are for viewing only.
          • Waiting for 4H/1H to agree before buying: made stock signals worse across 1,820 trades and didn't help crypto.
          • Classic indicators (14-day RSI and stochastics, Bollinger bounces, EMA/Supertrend trend, new-high breakouts, turn of the month): no reliable edge.
          • InvestAnswers ribbon and ATR line as signals: no clear edge on their own, so they're a chart view.
          • "100% win" backtests: tuning the ATR model per stock the way InvestAnswers' demo does always picks "only sell in profit". That keeps a 100% win rate, but from 2023 it made +144% vs +293% for just holding (median of 29 stocks), with the same drops. Losing positions aren't sold, they're held.
          • Confluence model (rebuilt from your UMAC chart: Bollinger-band re-entries): trailed holding on stocks; on crypto its "only sell above cost" version left coins about 50% under cost.
          • Ranking stocks by recent strength: about +3% a year, but not reliable (t 0.8).
          • Buying after strong earnings: too weak to use.
          • Sell-off → base → breakout → buy the pullback (Weinstein "Stage 2" / Wyckoff): about 3,000 cases in today's S&P 500 since 2005. Buying on the breakout, the retest or the first dip did no better than holding the same stocks on an average day, and won 40–49% of trades. Too few clean cases in crypto to judge.
          • Copying Congress: 22,800 reported trades by 266 members since 2018. Once a buy was public, the stock trailed the S&P 500 by about 1% over the next 6 months (t −2.7 since 2021). Buys by 2 or more members in the same month did a little better (+0.75% over the next month in both periods), but it faded after that.

          Limits

          Past results don't guarantee future ones. The tests used today's watchlist, which leaves out companies and coins that later failed, and that flatters the results. Averages hide plenty of losers: even Strong Buys were down 5 days later roughly 4 to 5 times in 10. Personal research tool, not financial advice.
